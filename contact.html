A pocket guide for the newly arrived

Make a life out here.
One day at a time.

Dry Creek isn't much to look at from the train, but give it a day or two. Take your time: jobs run in real time, good gear is earned, and Ironjaw is not a beginner's problem.

01 — PUT IN A SHIFT

Work takes a minute

Take one job at a time. It runs for 60 seconds, even if you leave the tab or reload. Jobs favor different materials: delivery brings timber, ranch work brings hide, and saloon duty brings scrap and herbs. Material percentages are average units per job (120% means 1.2 on average); recipes show what each material is needed for.

02 — GROW YOUR TRADE

Level up & unlock

Every level adds another job to your daily limit. Levels have no cap: after reaching level 14 and unlocking the current jobs, crafts, and homestead, you can keep working and earning XP for as long as you like. Advanced survey, foundry, and glasswork jobs appear only after their story chapters open. The Outfitters sell only trail biscuits and healing tonics; gear must be crafted or earned in the field.

03 — BUILD YOUR KIT

Craft, refine & equip

The satchel groups gear by slot and materials separately. Each Equip button names its destination and compares gains and losses against the currently worn item, including weapon damage and set bonuses. Fine gear needs finished materials: build an assay shed to smelt copper, a tanning shed to cure hide, then unlock the gunsmith bench, precision foundry, and Blackglass works as the story advances.

04 — FIND YOUR TRADE

Wear a set

Trailblazer gear balances every stat, Deadeye favors damage and aim, and Ironbark favors armor and dodge. Equip two pieces for the first bonus and four pieces for an additional bonus. Your character sheet tracks set progress.

05 — KEEP YOUR WORD

Daily errands

Complete three jobs, explore a trail, and finish the rotating side mission—gather materials, explore more trails, or win a gulch fight—to earn the daily bonus. Progress resets when you sleep, so unfinished errands forfeit that day's bonus.

06 — TAKE A CHANCE

Combat is part of the story

The sheriff's wanted-board chapter requires a Ridgeback coyote and Gulch road-agent; their clues lead to Ironjaw after chapter 6. His ironheart powers the forge and railsteel needed for the Railbreaker rifle, which is required to face Vane in chapter 10. After Vane, chapter rewards and newly opened jobs provide rail plans and cobalt for a Stormline carbine. That rifle is required for the high-cut outlaws and Cinderback. Cinderback's heartstone powers the precision foundry and the Sunbreak rifle needed against the Rail Warden. The Warden's badge unlocks the glassworks and Dawnseeker rifle for the Glass Titan. Enemies stay locked until their chapter and required weapon are ready.

07 — CLAIM YOUR PLACE

Build the homestead

Sign the Bell deed, buy planks, stone, and nails at the Builder's Yard, then build a cabin and choose your improvements. Two larger home expansions, the gunsmith bench, Cinderback precision foundry, and Blackglass works open later in the story. Disabled building cards list each missing material, level, plot, chapter, or prerequisite.

08 — FOLLOW THE LEDGER

A solo frontier story

The Vane Ledger campaign follows Sardonix's family debt from a crumbling cabin to a railroad conspiracy, then out into the high pass. Fourteen chapters remember your jobs, trails, crafting, construction, and victories, with new enemies gated behind story chapters and crafted weapons. The Achievements tab tracks your progress and ends with a complete-ledger honor for the current campaign content. Earning it does not stop jobs, XP, or levels; keep making a life on the frontier. Unfinished chapter requirements persist between days.
